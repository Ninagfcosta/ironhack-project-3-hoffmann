# Client Recommendation Memo

**To:** Head of Human Resources  
**Company:** Fictional HR Recruitment Company  
**Prepared by:** Marc Tanguy  
**Subject:** GDPR First-Pass Compliance Assessment – AI Resume Screening System

---

## Bottom Line

**Recommendation: GO WITH CONDITIONS**

The proposed AI-assisted recruitment system may proceed, provided that several GDPR compliance measures are implemented before processing applicant data in production. While the overall objective is legitimate, the current design presents risks relating to automated profiling, indefinite data retention, and international data transfers.

---

## Top Three Recommended Actions

### 1. Conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

Before deployment, complete a DPIA covering the AI ranking process, the potential impact on applicants' rights, and the safeguards implemented to reduce those risks. Given the use of AI in recruitment and the possibility of profiling, this should be treated as a priority.

### 2. Establish processor agreements and international transfer safeguards

Execute Data Processing Agreements with all processors involved, including the Applicant Tracking System provider, AI screening provider, and US-based cloud vendor. Where personal data is transferred outside the EEA, implement an appropriate transfer mechanism, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (where applicable) or Standard Contractual Clauses supported by a Transfer Impact Assessment.

### 3. Strengthen transparency, retention, and human oversight

Replace indefinite resume storage with a documented retention policy that reflects recruitment needs and applicable employment law. Update the privacy notice to explain the AI-assisted screening process, and ensure recruiters perform meaningful human review before decisions that significantly affect applicants are made.

---

## Residual Risks

Even after implementing these recommendations, several risks remain.

First, AI models may introduce unintended bias that disadvantages certain groups of applicants. Continuous monitoring and periodic validation should therefore be incorporated into the recruitment process.

Second, applicants may challenge automated profiling decisions or request detailed explanations of how their application was assessed. Internal procedures should be established to respond consistently to these requests.

Finally, international transfers to US-based service providers remain subject to evolving regulatory and legal developments. The organization should regularly review its transfer mechanisms and processor agreements to ensure continued compliance.

---

## Closing Statement

Based on the information currently available, I recommend proceeding with the project only after the above compliance measures have been implemented. This assessment represents a first-pass GDPR review and should not be considered a formal legal opinion. Final implementation should be reviewed with the organization's legal counsel and Data Protection Officer before processing applicant data in production.
